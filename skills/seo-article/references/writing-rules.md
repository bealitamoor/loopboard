# Writing rules for SEO articles

Write for the person who typed the query. Search engines follow readers.

## Voice
- Plain, direct, specific. The way a knowledgeable colleague would explain it across a desk.
- Answer first. The first paragraph gives the answer or the result; the rest earns it.
- Short sentences and paragraphs; one idea each. Headings say what the section answers.
- Use "you" for the reader and "I" or "we" only for experience that is real.
- Examples, steps and numbers from real use beat general statements.

## Facts
- Every statistic, date, price and claim needs a source you can link, or comes from the user's own data. Otherwise
  write `[fill: <what number, from where>]`. Never invent a figure, a study, an expert quote or a ranking.
- Say when something depends ("on Android 14 and newer"), and when you have not tested it.
- Do not promise results ("rank first", "double your traffic").
- Dates: state when the article was written or checked if the content ages.

## Words and patterns to avoid
Game-changer, ultimate guide, in today's fast-paced world, in the ever-evolving landscape, unlock, leverage,
delve, dive in, comprehensive guide, "it's important to note", "when it comes to", "look no further",
"in conclusion", rhetorical-question openers, stacked adjectives, hype exclamation marks, filler intros that restate
the title, FAQ sections full of questions nobody asks.

## On-page basics
- Title about 60 characters or fewer, keyword near the front, written for a human.
- Meta description about 155 characters, says what the reader gets.
- One H1; H2s for sections; H3s only when needed.
- URL slug short, lowercase, hyphens.
- Primary keyword in the title, the first paragraph and one H2, used naturally. No stuffing.
- 2 to 5 internal links to real pages with descriptive anchor text; outbound links only to sources that help.
- Images: real, useful, with alt text that describes them; no stock filler.
- If the page answers a question concisely, put the answer in a short paragraph or list near the top.

## Before you hand it over
- [ ] Answer is in the first paragraph.
- [ ] No banned phrases; reads like a person.
- [ ] Every number sourced or `[fill: ...]`.
- [ ] Internal links point to pages that exist.
- [ ] Title, meta description, slug, alt text written.
