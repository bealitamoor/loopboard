# Writing rules for social posts (and any public copy)

The goal is a post that sounds like one specific person wrote it on a good day.

## Voice
- Write the way the person talks. If you have a past post, a bio or a voice note, match its sentence length, its
  punctuation and its level of formality. If you have none, ask for two samples before drafting.
- First person, concrete nouns, active verbs. One idea per post.
- Short paragraphs. A line break is fine; a wall of one-line paragraphs for effect is not.
- Start with the thing itself: the result, the mistake, the number, the question. Not a warm-up.
- End when the point is made. No recap, no "let me know your thoughts" unless the question is real.

## Facts
- Use only numbers, names and quotes the user gave you or a file contains. Where a number should go and you have
  none, write `[fill: <what number, from where>]`. Never round up, estimate or "about" your way to a figure.
- Do not claim results, customers, rankings or press that you cannot point to.
- Real names, handles and tags only when the user supplied them.

## Words to avoid
Game-changer, thrilled / excited / proud to announce, in today's fast-paced world, unlock, supercharge, elevate,
leverage, delve, dive in, deep dive, journey, landscape, ecosystem (unless literal), revolutionize, cutting-edge,
seamless, robust, "here's the thing", "let that sink in", "this is a reminder that", "not just X, but Y",
"X is dead", "stop doing X", rhetorical-question openers, em-dash chains, emoji as bullet points, hype
exclamation marks.

## Structure patterns that work
- Story: what I tried, what happened (with the real number), what I would do now.
- Lesson: the rule in one line, then the one example that taught it.
- Build in public: what shipped this week, what broke, what is next.
- Question: a specific question you actually want answered, with the context that makes it answerable.

## Channel notes
- **LinkedIn**: put links in the first comment, not in the post body (the post says "link in the first comment" or
  nothing). 1,000 to 1,300 characters is plenty. First two lines must stand alone before "see more". 3 hashtags is
  usually right, never more than 5, at the end.
- **X / Threads / Bluesky**: one post, one idea; thread only when the steps genuinely need it. Link in the post is
  fine. 0 to 2 hashtags.
- **Instagram / Facebook**: caption first line carries the hook; hashtags at the end, 3 to 5 maximum; link goes in
  bio or comments, as the platform requires.
- **YouTube / short video descriptions**: first 150 characters carry the hook and the keyword; chapters if long.
- Alt text on every image, written as a plain description.

## Hashtags
3 to 5 at most. Specific beats broad. Never invent a tag that nobody uses; if unsure, leave it out.

## Before you hand it over
- [ ] No banned words or hype patterns above.
- [ ] Every number is from a source or is a `[fill: ...]`.
- [ ] Reads like a person, aloud, without stumbling.
- [ ] Links placed per channel rules; hashtags 3 to 5 at most.
- [ ] Alt text written for every image.
